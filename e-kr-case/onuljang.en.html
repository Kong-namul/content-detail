A new way to shop for groceries, Onuljang App Renewal

Client
Onuljang Inc.
Launch
2026.03
Scope
Mobile App, Renewal, UX/UI
Platform
iOS, Android
KEY VISUAL · 16:9

We redrew every step of Onuljang, from the first screen to checkout, so that grocery shopping begins not with filling a cart but with imagining tonight's dinner table.

GOAL BANNER · 21:9

PROJECT GOAL

From an app you search to an app you browse

Onuljang grew quickly on 30-minute fresh food delivery, yet the app remained a tool for searching, adding what you need and leaving. This renewal set out to make people stay longer, discover new ingredients and recipes, and come back again next week.

POINT 01

Shopping by discovery

We offer inspiration before the search bar

The home screen is filled with seasonal ingredients and tonight's table suggestions, and every ingredient a recipe needs can be added in one tap. Even shoppers who haven't decided what to buy can settle on a meal after a few scrolls.

HOME DISCOVERY · 16:9
POINT 02

Trust in 30-minute delivery

We make the waiting time visible

From the moment an order is placed until it reaches the door, packing, dispatch and arrival appear on a single timeline. When a substitute is needed, we show it with a photo and let the customer choose first, so a fast promise becomes a trustworthy experience.

DELIVERY TRACKING · 16:9
POINT 03

Regulars who return

Your usual items come first

Based on purchase history, a Favorites shelf gathers frequently bought items and suggests reordering just as they run out. Instead of a complicated tier chart, loyalty rewards appear as small notes in the cart, so they build up without any effort.

REPEAT ORDER · 16:9

DESIGN CONCEPT

Freshness you can feel on screen

We aimed for screens where the ingredients catch your eye first, as if you were standing at a market stall. Three design languages (keyword, color and icon) tie the whole app together, from home to checkout.

KEYWORD

A screen where freshness shows

We stripped away decoration and made room for photos and white space. Product images are cut out large without backgrounds, and the arrival time and origin sit right beside each product name, so freshness can be read as well as seen.

KEYWORD VISUAL · 16:9

COLOR SYSTEM

Six colors drawn from vegetables

We picked six colors from the ingredients that end up in a basket: the green of spinach, the orange of carrots, the purple of eggplant. Color is used only to separate categories, while buttons and body text stay a calm neutral so the products stand out.

COLOR PALETTE · 16:9

ICON STYLE

Rounded lines that fit the hand

We redrew more than 120 icons with thick, round-ended lines. Sized for a fingertip and softened at the edges, they stay crisp and friendly even on a small screen.

ICON SET · 16:9

PROCESS 01

The cart should work with one hand

So it can be used on a commute or in a kitchen where both hands are busy, we gathered the main actions in the thumb zone at the bottom of the screen. Usability tests checked that adjusting quantity, adding and heading to checkout all work one-handed, and we tuned button sizes and spacing along the way.

ONE-HAND UI · 16:9

PROCESS 02

Even sold-out items get a kind word

Fresh food sells out often, so rather than treat that as a weakness we turned it into a chance to help. Next to a sold-out product we show similar ingredients and the next restock time, and we kept the flow short so a restock alert puts the item in the cart the moment it returns.

SOLD OUT FLOW · 16:9

PROCESS 03

Small movements add a little joy

An item lightly flies into the cart when you add it, and a delivery box opens when an order is complete. Each motion finishes within 0.4 seconds, so speed is never lost and shopping still feels like a pleasure.
